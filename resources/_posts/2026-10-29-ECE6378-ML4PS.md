## Machine Learning with Applications in Power Systems (ML4PS)
ECE6378 is a graduate-level course, first offered at the University of Houston (UH) in 2026 Fall.


#### Course Description:
This course introduces fundamental machine learning (ML) methods and their applications to electric power systems. It begins with selected electrical engineering, transmission grid, and distribution grid foundations so that students from different backgrounds can develop a common vocabulary. It then progresses from grid measurements and data preparation, to classical supervised learning, deep learning, graph-based learning, time-series learning, and unsupervised learning, and finally to ML-assisted and ML-embedded power system optimization.

The course emphasizes not only how an ML model is formulated and trained, but also:
* why a particular model is suitable for a particular task;
* how data resolution and measurement sources affect model design;
* how training, validation, and testing should be separated;
* how physical laws and operational constraints can be used to check predictions;
* how ML can accelerate or be embedded within optimization; and
* when a simpler or physics-based method may be preferable to a more complex ML model.

<p style="font-size:1.1em; font-weight:700; color:#C8102E; border-bottom:2px solid #eee; padding-bottom:4px; margin-top:1.4em;">Power Grid Foundations (Lectures 2 - 4):</p>
**Lecture 2 - Electrical Engineering Fundamentals:** voltage, current, power, and energy; Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws as physics checks for grid data; AC waveforms, RMS values, and phasors; impedance; active, reactive, and apparent power; three-phase systems, one-line diagrams, positive-sequence models, and the per-unit system.
* <a class="" target="_blank" href="https://youtu.be/WyUfMdlwMTw">L2 Video on YouTube</a>

**Lecture 3 - Transmission Grid Fundamentals:** grid structure and North American interconnections; generators, step-up transformers, circuit breakers, transmission lines, substations, shunt capacitors, FACTS devices, and phase shifters; graph models of the IEEE 24-bus, PJM, and synthetic Texas grids; operating time scales, frequency control, SCADA/EMS, wholesale electricity markets, and ML opportunities.
* <a class="" target="_blank" href="https://youtu.be/MKtkKyvFMhA">L3 Video on YouTube</a>

**Lecture 4 - Distribution Grid Fundamentals:** feeders, laterals, service transformers, and the grid-to-home interface; utility poles, underground cables, and duct banks; electric arcs and protective devices (reclosers, fuses, sectionalizers, surge arresters); voltage regulators; feeder graphs and configurations; microgrids, retail electricity markets, distribution applications, and ML opportunities.
* <a class="" target="_blank" href="https://youtu.be/DAQOMJoNAy0">L4 Video on YouTube</a>

<p style="font-size:1.1em; font-weight:700; color:#C8102E; border-bottom:2px solid #eee; padding-bottom:4px; margin-top:1.4em;">Deep Neural Networks (Lectures 16 - 18):</p>
**Lecture 16 - DNN Model: MLP & Forward Propagation:** universal approximation, neurons and activation functions, MLP architecture and forward propagation, loss functions (MSE, cross-entropy, weighted loss), and a frequency stability prediction example for frequency-constrained OPF.
* <a class="" target="_blank" href="https://youtu.be/v_f_pPX1u7E">L16 Video on YouTube</a>

**Lecture 17 - DNN Training & Backpropagation:** gradient descent, chain rule, computation graph and backpropagation, and a complete hand-calculated training step (forward pass, backward pass, and parameter update) on a small MLP, followed by training across multiple epochs.
* <a class="" target="_blank" href="https://youtu.be/4HpvsWPb2EY">L17 Video on YouTube</a>

**Lecture 18 - DNN Tuning:** learning rate schedules, vanishing and exploding gradients, batch / stochastic / mini-batch gradient descent, adaptive optimizers (AdaGrad, RMSprop, Adam), and regularization and normalization (dropout, weight decay, early stopping, BatchNorm, LayerNorm).

#### Prerequisites:
Basic understanding of electrical circuits, AC and DC systems, basic math, and linear algebra. Prior coursework in power systems is helpful but not required; lectures 2 - 4 provide the power system fundamentals needed for this course.


#### Software Requirements:
This course uses Python 3.12 with Jupyter Notebook. A class Conda environment file is provided with the course materials.
