# Abstract
Accurate prediction of frequency dynamics under varying network topologies is essential for rapid frequency security assessment, whereas repeated electromagnetic transient (EMT) simulations are computationally intensive. This paper develops a topology-aware graph neural network with a radial basis function Kolmogorov–Arnold network (RBF-KAN) regression head, termed RK-GNN, to predict frequency nadir and maximum absolute rate of change of frequency (RoCoF). Scenarios combine load scaling, regional load redistribution, and generator redispatch with intact, N−1, and N−2 transmission topologies. After checks for network connectivity, generator reference limits, and numerical consistency, the retained scenarios are simulated in PSCAD to construct the reference dataset. The RK-GNN is evaluated against a conventional deep neural network (DNN) and a baseline GNN. On the IEEE 39-bus system, the RK-GNN reduces the mean absolute errors (MAEs) of nadir and RoCoF by 48% and 59% relative to the DNN and by 42% and 61% relative to the baseline GNN. Incorporating the bridge indicator and disturbance hop distance yields further MAE reductions of 27% and 54%. These results show that the proposed topology-aware architecture provides accurate prediction of both frequency metrics under varying topologies and substantially reduces the need for repeated EMT simulations.
