# Abstract
Hosting capacity is commonly assessed with steady-state optimization, which leaves the dynamic response of the data center and the grid at the resulting operating point untested. This paper assesses candidate data center capacities by coupling a unit commitment (UC) model with electromagnetic transient (EMT) simulation. During the capacity search, the UC model determines the economic dispatch for each tested data center capacity, and the resulting operating points are tested for settling with the generators committed online by the UC model and after a selected synchronous generator outage. Three nested capacities are obtained: the optimization-based steady-state capacity, the dynamic pre-outage capacity, and the post-outage dynamic capacity, each bounded by the previous one. On a test system with a detailed data center model, both dynamic capacities fall well below the optimization capacity, and the ranking of connection locations changes with the assessment stage and with the outage. In the most severe outage case at each candidate bus, the capacity is about 30% below the steady-state capacity. Thus, the dynamic response of the data center and the grid should be part of the hosting capacity assessment, and the comparison of connection locations should be made on the post-outage dynamic capacity.
