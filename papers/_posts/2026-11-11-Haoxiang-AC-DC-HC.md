# Abstract
A screening procedure based on direct current (DC) power flow estimates the data center hosting capacity of a candidate bus, termed the DC-screened capacity, and selects the generator commitment that supports it. This paper assesses that estimate under the alternating current (AC) network model. With the selected commitment fixed, an AC optimal power flow reoptimizes dispatch and voltages to find the largest data center load the bus can carry, termed the AC-assessed capacity. On a synthetic Texas system with 123 buses, the DC-screened capacity exceeds the AC-assessed capacity at 113 buses. Across all 123, the ratio of DC-screened to AC-assessed capacity has a median of 143% (a 43% overestimate) and a maximum of 1,070%. At these 113 buses, the DC-screened capacity violates a branch apparent power limit or a generator reactive power limit in the AC model, two limits the DC model omits. The overestimate changes decisions: a 1-GW project passes DC screening at every bus but exceeds the AC-assessed capacity at 37, and the bus ranked first by DC screening has 205.5 MW less AC-assessed capacity than the bus ranked second. Reporting the AC-assessed capacity beside each DC-screened capacity lets planners reject oversized projects or choose better sites.
